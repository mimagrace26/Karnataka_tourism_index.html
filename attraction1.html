Mysuru Palace

Introduction

Mysore Palace is the sparkling jewel of Karnataka's royal legacy. With its magnificent Indo-Saracenic architecture and vibrant events, this palace draws visitors worldwide seeking history, art, and the spirit of Mysore's celebrated Dasara festival.

Brief History

The palace you see today is actually the fourth version built on this spot. For centuries, the royal family lived in a grand wooden palace, but it kept getting damaged by weather and lightning. The final straw came in 1897, when a massive fire broke out during a royal wedding and completely destroyed the building. Instead of giving up, Maharani Vani Vilas Sannidhana hired British architect Henry Irwin to design a fireproof palace that couldn't be destroyed. It took 15 years and over 42 lakh rupees to finish, wrapping up in 1912. Irwin blended Hindu, Islamic, Rajput, and European styles using solid granite, deep-red marble domes, and carved rosewood.

Fun Facts

•The legendary 200-kg Golden Throne is kept in high-security storage year-round and is reassembled like a giant puzzle using ancient structural joints only during the 10 days of Dasara.

•The iconic octagonal pavilion (Kalyana Mantapa) features a soaring stained-glass ceiling custom-designed and imported directly from Glasgow, Scotland

•The entire palace exterior is wired with over 97,000 incandescent bulbs that are replaced individually by hand using rope harness technicians

How To Reach

By Air: Mysore Airport (12 km) connects regionally; Kempegowda International Airport Bengaluru is ~170 km away.

By Train/Road: Frequent express trains and KSRTC bus services operate directly between Bengaluru and Mysuru central stations.

Entry fee and timings

•Timings: 10:00 AM – 5:30 PM (Daily)

•Adult Entry Fee: ₹100 (Indians) | ₹100 (Foreigners)

Things To Keep In Mind

•Entry tickets are required; footwear must be deposited at designated counters before entering the palace interior.

•Cameras and photography are prohibited inside the main palace building (permitted in outer grounds).

•Main walking routes are wheelchair accessible.

•Visit early morning or mid-afternoon on weekdays to avoid long queues.
