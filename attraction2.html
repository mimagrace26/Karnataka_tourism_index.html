Lalbagh Botanical Garden

Introduction

Nestled in the heart of Bengaluru, Lalbagh Botanical Garden is far more than just a patch of green in the middle of a bustling metropolis. Home to over 1,850 plant species from across the globe, it serves as an active center for botanical research, horticulture education, and urban conservation.

Brief History

Lalbagh was commissioned in 1760 by Hyder Ali, ruler of Mysore, as a 40-acre private estate modeled after traditional Persian Charbagh layouts. His son, Tipu Sultan, expanded the collection by importing species from Persia, France, and Afghanistan, establishing early plant exchange networks. Following the fall of Srirangapatna in 1799, the British government took administration of the grounds and converted them into a state botanical station in 1856. Supervised by directors from London's Royal Botanic Gardens at Kew, Lalbagh became a research center for acclimatizing foreign crops. In 1889, construction finished on the Glass House, built with cast iron imported from Glasgow, Scotland, and modeled on London's Crystal Palace.

Fun Facts

•Tipu Sultan was so obsessed with botany that he used diplomatic immunity to import exotic seeds from France, South Africa, and Persia

•Lalbagh houses rare specimens of the Ginkgo Biloba tree—often referred to as a "living fossil"—a species that has remained completely unchanged for over 270 million years, predating the dinosaurs.

•The central Glass House was directly inspired by London's Crystal Palace; its entire cast-iron framework was forged by the Macfarlane Foundry in Glasgow, shipped across oceans, and assembled piece-by-piece in Bengaluru

How To Reach

By Metro: Green Line Metro stops directly at the "Lalbagh Metro Station" opposite West Gate.

By Road/Air: Easily accessible by BMTC city buses, cabs, and auto-rickshaws; ~38 km from Kempegowda International Airport.

Things To Keep In Mind

•Morning and evening non-fee hours are ideal for peaceful walks and avoiding vehicle traffic.

•Plastic bags and disposal items are strictly prohibited inside garden grounds to maintain cleanliness.

•Electric buggy rides are available inside for senior citizens and persons with physical disabilities.

•Parking spaces are available near the main gates for a nominal vehicle fee.
